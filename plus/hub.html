Concept note · The Hub

A shared‑services backbone for independent media.
The Hub.

The Hub is Plus's flagship shared-services programme — sustained, hands-on technical capacity delivered directly into the operations of independent media. Seed-funded by the Knight Foundation and the Google News Initiative, launched in December 2025 to replace and substantially expand the long-running ICFJ Knight Fellowships model.

01 Why a Hub

Not another training programme — a delivery system.

The Hub exists because we recognise that what independent media need isn't another training programme. It's sustained, hands-on technical capacity delivered directly into their operations — enterprise-grade engineering, audience and analytics work, tailored to each organisation's actual stack and constraints.

We provide enterprise-level support across publishing technology, security, audience development and business operations. We work in cohorts, so partner newsrooms also learn from one another — but the engineering is one-on-one, on your infrastructure, and anything we build stays yours.

02 Three foundation layers

What the Hub covers.

01 Market validation

Does the product fit the market?

Audience research, competitive analysis, niche definition, value-proposition design, positioning and pricing work. We help partners answer the questions that determine whether their journalism can sustain itself: who is the audience, what do they actually need, and what will they pay for.

02 Audience development

Building real relationships beyond the pageview.

First-party data foundations, newsletter and membership infrastructure, event tracking and custom dashboards on your own analytics stack — so editorial decisions are backed by usable data. Distribution work across search, AI platforms, social, syndication and partnerships, with author signals and technical SEO built in.

03 Publisher stack

Publishing technology that actually works.

Site performance, availability and mobile speed; CMS architecture (or working around it when it can't be modified); discoverability infrastructure; schema and SEO; security baselines and infrastructure monitoring. Where build work needs technical involvement on your side we partner — where you'd rather we drive the setup, we do that with access to the relevant parts of your stack.

03 How a cycle works

16 weeks. One scoped work plan. One cohort.

i.

Technical audit

We start with a technical audit of your site and a scoping conversation to confirm what we've found and understand your priorities. From that we build a scoped work plan — and agree on access levels through a data-handling agreement before kick-off.

ii.

One-on-one build

Implementation is a mix of one-on-one engineering — improving your systems directly, on your infrastructure — and guided group sessions across the cohort, so you can maintain what we build together once we step back.

iii.

Cohort learning

You join a cohort of publishers in a similar position for peer-to-peer troubleshooting of shared challenges. Plan for about 1–2 hours a week during two of the four months, with action items in between — for both our teams.

iv.

Measure & remeasure

We baseline a readiness score across six dimensions — site performance, discoverability, analytics, distribution, revenue infrastructure, publishing platform — then re-measure at end of engagement and three months on.

What you walk away with

A dashboard tied to your editorial questions. Schema and SEO improvements live on your site. A performance monitor running on your infrastructure. Baseline measurement against the metrics that matter.

i

Your stack, yours

Everything we build runs on your infrastructure and stays yours. No vendor lock-in. No proprietary dependencies.

ii

Closed gaps

A measurable delta on the readiness score — which gaps closed, which remain, and a credible path forward.

iii

A cohort

Peers in similar positions, contactable beyond the engagement, working through the same publisher problems.

04 Cohorts

Who the Hub is for.

The 2025 inaugural cohort focused on investigative info-integrity researchers across four continents. 2026 brings four additional cohort tracks:

  • Journalistic content creators — newsletter writers, podcasters, video journalists, indie info providers building loyal audiences.
  • Exiled media — newsrooms operating from outside the countries they cover, with all the security, distribution and audience challenges that involves.
  • Feminist organisations responding to tfGBV — civic groups and newsrooms working on technology-facilitated gender-based violence.
  • Local and grassroots media — the small newsrooms that anchor local information ecosystems and are most exposed to capture and collapse.

The current Publishing Technologies track also runs as part of Google's Global News Gaps Project, in English, with other languages to follow.
