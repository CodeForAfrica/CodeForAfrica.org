Introducing Plus.

CfA co-founded Plus with the International Center for Journalists (ICFJ) and PROTO in November 2024. The three partners consolidated and merged operations through 2025. The European Journalism Centre (EJC) joined as the alliance's fourth constituent partner in June 2026. Together, we cultivate civic intelligence.

01 A full-circle moment

From incubation to alliance.

CfA was born in Kenya in 2012 to give ordinary citizens actionable information, by liberating data and building digital tools so they could hold power to account. It pioneered the continent's first open data platforms, fact-checking newsrooms, drone/satellite/sensor journalism pilots, and feminist AI initiatives.

ICFJ helped seed those very first CfA labs, when civic technology was still a fringe idea. Thirteen years later, the relationship has come full circle: CfA provides ICFJ with a new operational home at its Cape Town headquarters, while ICFJ's networks, training infrastructure and capstone programmes flow through Plus.

03 Four focus areas

The information-integrity threats we organise around.

i.

Crisis

Helping journalists, civic groups and citizens navigate moments of acute information crisis — elections, conflicts, climate disasters, public-health emergencies — where the cost of a poorly-informed public is paid in lives, livelihoods and democratic legitimacy.

ii.

Capture

Defending media independence against state capture, oligarchic capture, platform capture and ideological capture. Building economic, legal and operational resilience for newsrooms in hostile environments.

iii.

Access

Expanding access to trustworthy information — across languages, geographies, platforms and modalities — and to the tools, training and capital that independent media and civic-tech organisations need.

iv.

Dominion

Contesting the rapid consolidation of information infrastructure into the hands of a few platforms, AI labs and politically-aligned media empires. Building open, auditable, publicly-accountable alternatives.

02 The AI Sandbox

CfA's applied-AI stack, going global.

Built by CfA to give African civic-tech organisations, newsrooms and human-rights groups practical access to applied AI — not as a buzzword but as working infrastructure. Through Plus, the Sandbox now scales as a global "innovation-in-a-box".

ComputeLlama · Mistral · Gemma · Qwen, with regional inference
InteractionPromptForge — prompt engineering & orchestration
ExpertDomain-specific Custom GPTs and Spaces
OperationsTemplateForge — workflow & template layer
DeploymentThe Commons — hosting & distribution

Flagship cohort: the Gendered AI Innovation Challenge (GAIC), anchored by IDRC Canada — 107 women's-rights organisations across 22 African countries, 47 concept papers, and a portfolio of working tools (Feminist Forensics, ClimateCheck, TrustList, RoboShield, PromiseTracker, Wakili AI). The Sandbox is now building IFCN Shield — a global AI-driven crisis-management toolkit for frontline fact-checking newsrooms operating under harassment, cyberattack, funding shocks, raids, war and exile. Built with the International Fact-Checking Network and twelve case-study partners, Shield packages 14 threat domains, 72 operational triggers and 22 controls into a continuity-planning web app, with downloadable BCP and hibernation templates, ephemeral browser-only state, and a one-tap panic-wipe.

03 The iLAB, replicated

Africa's forensic newsroom, now a global model.

CfA's iLAB is Africa's flagship forensic information-integrity newsroom — the operational backbone of investigations that have exposed coordinated FIMI campaigns, surveillance procurement, financial corruption, climate disinformation and tfGBV networks across the continent. Through Plus, the iLAB is being replicated in Latin America, Europe, South Asia and MENA, anchored to ICFJ's Disarming Disinformation programme (Scripps Howard Foundation).

Case study · 2025

ARIJ Cyberstorm

The DD-supported ARIJ "Cyberstorm" investigation — produced in partnership with CfA — won the WAN-IFRA Asian Media Award for Best Climate Impact Story. It exposed a Gulf-account climate-denial disinformation network operating across Arabic-language social media. Exactly the kind of cross-regional, multi-language, forensically-rigorous work the iLAB model is built to make routine.

04 CivicSignal

Mapping the media ecosystems that shape public discourse.

CivicSignal is Africa's largest non-profit media monitoring and mapping initiative. Its two flagship products — MediaCloud (tracking content) and MediaData (mapping media ownership, audiences and the people who control them) — are used by development agencies, regulators and researchers across the continent. Through Plus, CivicSignal is now expanding well beyond Africa.

Expanded mapping

MediaData is extending its mapping of media ecosystems into the Middle East, the Caucasus and South Asia — building open, comparable datasets on who owns, funds and influences media in regions where ownership transparency has been historically opaque.

Mapping the creators

CivicSignal is also building the first systematic mapping of news creators — newsletter writers, video journalists, podcasters and indie info providers — in partnership with Project C and the Center for News, Technology & Innovation (CNTI). Regional lists of standout creators are rolling out across Africa, Latin America, MENA and South & Southeast Asia, alongside parallel research studies into how indie info providers build credibility, revenue and audience.

Reuters DNR · Africa leg

CivicSignal anchors the Africa leg of the Reuters Institute's Digital News Report — the world's most-cited annual study of news consumption — covering Kenya, Nigeria, Morocco and South Africa. The 2026 Africa launch takes place this week, hosted by CfA in partnership with the Reuters Institute at the University of Oxford.

China In The World

CivicSignal also powers the African research stream of China In The World — tracking PRC narrative influence, surveillance-technology exports and infrastructure financing across African media ecosystems.

07 What stays the same

For our partners, funders and audiences — CfA continues.

The CfA brand and its programmes continue. ANCIR, PesaCheck, sensors.AFRICA, africanDRONE, openAFRICA, sourceAFRICA, Academy.AFRICA and the iLAB all continue to operate under their existing names, with their existing teams and partners.

The change is not that CfA disappears into a global org. The change is that CfA's programmes now have a global delivery system behind them — and that CfA's engineering, product and operational capacity is now serving a worldwide network of partner organisations.
